Title: NC Utility Shutoff or Essential Services Resource
URL: https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_42/GS_42-42.1.html
Source type: nc_general_statute
Issue category: utility_shutoff

Overview
North Carolina law restricts a landlord's ability to shut off utility services to force payment or make a tenant leave.

Rule on landlord-billed utility services
Under N.C. Gen. Stat. 42-42.1, a lessor may charge for certain utility services under a written rental agreement, including:
- water or sewer service
- electric service
- natural gas service
- electricity or natural gas used by a central system

However, under subsection (b), the lessor may not disconnect or terminate the lessee's:
- electric service
- water or sewer service
- natural gas service
- receipt of the benefits of the use of a central system

The landlord may not do this because of the tenant's nonpayment of the amount due for those utility services.

Related tenant-protection principle
North Carolina courts and court guidance also treat utility shutoff as an example of unlawful self-help eviction.
Landlords generally cannot force tenants out by turning off utilities instead of going through court.

Practical implication
If a landlord is billing a tenant for covered utility services, the landlord's remedy for nonpayment is not to shut the service off.
The landlord must use lawful collection or eviction procedures instead of self-help.

Key takeaways
- A landlord may bill for certain utilities if the written rental agreement allows it.
- A landlord generally may not shut off those services because the tenant did not pay the utility charges.
- Utility shutoff used to force a tenant out is also a strong indicator of unlawful self-help eviction.
